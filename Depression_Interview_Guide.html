01

Best AI/CV project — problem, approach, what you built

Q: Explain the best AI/CV project you have built. What problem were you solving, what was your approach, and what exactly did you build yourself?
My best AI project is a conversational depression-detection system (NLP/ML, not computer vision). The problem was to estimate depression risk from normal chat with an LLM persona, without asking about depression directly, then produce a BDI-II style score (0–63) and main symptoms.

My approach: persona replies go through keyword patterns plus sentence embeddings (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) against 21 BDI symptoms, with negation checks, then a running score and optional early stop.

I built the matching/scoring pipeline, conversation flow, local/CPU-friendly config, and submission-style outputs myself. It is a full project implementation of an eRisk-style setup, not my thesis research.
02

Biggest technical challenges

Q: What were the biggest technical challenges or constraints you faced? How did you solve them?
The hardest part was inferring symptoms from everyday language without direct mental-health questions, so keyword-only matching gave many false hits. I combined embeddings with patterns and added negation handling so lines like “I’m not sad” do not count as sadness.

Running models locally on CPU was another constraint, so I kept a small instruct-model path instead of a heavy setup. Deciding when to stop the chat was also tricky: too early meant a weak score, too late meant long runs. I used a confidence threshold plus a minimum number of turns.

I also needed the score to be explainable, so I tracked per-symptom signals instead of one opaque number.
03

Novelty

Q: Did you develop or discover anything novel in your project?
I would not claim a new research algorithm or paper. What is mine is the end-to-end system: persona conversation, embedding-plus-keyword symptom matching, BDI-style scoring, early stopping, and submission outputs under local/CPU limits.

The useful part for me was learning how evaluation-style scoring fails in practice — especially negation and over-counting — and tightening the pipeline so results are more stable and easier to explain. So the novelty is in the integrated design and debugging of the detection flow, not inventing a new model architecture.
Say this if they push: “I am honest about scope. The value is a working, explainable pipeline I can defend end to end.”
